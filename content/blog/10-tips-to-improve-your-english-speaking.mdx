You can know thousands of words and every grammar rule in the book — and still freeze when it's time to speak. Speaking is a skill of its own, and like any skill, it improves with the right kind of practice. Here are ten strategies our tutors recommend most often.

## 1. Speak from day one, not "when you're ready"

The biggest myth in language learning is that speaking comes *after* studying. In reality, speaking **is** the studying. You will never feel 100% ready — start talking now, mistakes included. Every awkward sentence you produce today is one you'll say smoothly next month.

## 2. Practice shadowing

Shadowing means listening to a short audio clip — a podcast, a scene from a series, a YouTube video — and repeating what you hear *as you hear it*, imitating the rhythm, stress and intonation. Ten minutes a day trains your mouth and your ear at the same time. Choose speakers whose accent you want to acquire.

## 3. Think in English

Narrate your day silently: "I'm making coffee. I should answer that email. This bus is late again." It sounds silly, but it removes the translation step that slows most learners down. When you catch yourself translating from your native language, pause and rebuild the thought directly in English.

## 4. Learn phrases, not just words

Native speakers talk in chunks: *"to be honest"*, *"it turns out that"*, *"I was wondering if"*. Memorising ready-made phrases gives you building blocks you can deploy instantly in conversation, and they make you sound far more natural than perfectly assembled — but robotic — word-by-word sentences.

## 5. Record yourself

Once a week, record yourself speaking for two minutes on any topic. Listen back and note two things to improve — a sound you mispronounce, a filler word you overuse. Then record the same topic again. The discomfort fades quickly; the progress doesn't.

## 6. Make mistakes on purpose

Perfectionism is the enemy of fluency. Set yourself a rule: in every conversation, attempt at least one structure you're not sure about. If it's wrong, you'll get a correction and remember it forever. If it's right, you've just expanded your comfort zone.

## 7. Master the sounds your language doesn't have

Every language background has its classic difficulties in English — the *th* sounds, the difference between *ship* and *sheep*, word stress in long words. Identify **your** three hardest sounds and drill them deliberately for a few minutes a day. Targeted pronunciation work pays off faster than any other kind.

## 8. Build a speaking routine, not speaking marathons

Twenty minutes of conversation four times a week beats a three-hour session once a month. Consistency rewires your brain; intensity just tires it. Put speaking practice in your calendar like a gym session — same day, same time.

## 9. Prepare "islands" for conversations

An island is a topic you can talk about comfortably for a minute or two: your job, your city, a hobby, a film you loved. Prepare and polish five islands. They give you safe ground in any conversation and free your attention for the truly spontaneous parts.
