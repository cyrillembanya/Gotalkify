The DELF (Diplôme d'Études en Langue Française) is the most widely recognised French proficiency diploma in the world. Issued by the French Ministry of Education, it never expires — pass it once and it's yours for life. Here's how to prepare effectively.

## Understand the exam format

The DELF is offered at four levels — A1, A2, B1 and B2 — and every level tests the same four skills:

- **Compréhension orale** — listening to recordings and answering questions
- **Compréhension écrite** — reading comprehension
- **Production écrite** — writing tasks (letters, essays, opinion pieces)
- **Production orale** — a face-to-face speaking test with an examiner

Each paper is worth 25 points. You need **50/100 overall** to pass, with a minimum of **5/25 in every paper**. That last rule matters: you cannot compensate for a disastrous speaking test with a brilliant essay.

## Choose the right level

Don't guess — test. Take a past paper under exam conditions and see where you land. As a rule of thumb:

- **A2** suits learners who can handle everyday transactions and simple past-tense narration.
- **B1** requires you to hold your own in conversation, tell stories, and justify opinions. It's the level most often required for French naturalisation.
- **B2** demands structured argumentation, and is the usual requirement for French universities.

If you're between two levels, register for the lower one. A confident pass is worth more than a stressful near-miss.

## A 12-week preparation plan

### Weeks 1–2: diagnose

Sit one full past paper (you'll find them on the France Éducation International website). Score each section honestly. Your weakest paper gets double practice time from now on.

### Weeks 3–8: build skills paper by paper

- **Listening:** daily exposure is non-negotiable. Use RFI's *Journal en français facile* for A2–B1, regular French radio for B2. Listen once for gist, once for detail, then read the transcript.
- **Reading:** read one article a day from a French newspaper site. Summarise it in two French sentences — this doubles as writing practice.
- **Writing:** learn the *formats*. DELF writing tasks are predictable: formal letters, opinion essays, complaints. Master the connectors (*d'abord, en revanche, par conséquent, en somme*) and the polite formulas — they earn easy points.
- **Speaking:** this is where most candidates underperform, and where a tutor changes everything. You need live practice of the monologue and the interactive role-play, with correction.

### Weeks 9–11: simulate

One timed past paper per week, all four sections in one sitting. Review every mistake. For speaking, have your tutor run full mock orals with exam timing: preparation time, monologue, questions.

### Week 12: taper

No new material. Re-read your notes, review your connector lists, sleep well. Confidence is part of the grade.

## Insider tips from our tutors

1. **In the listening paper, read the questions first.** They tell you what to listen for.
2. **Respect the word counts in writing.** Under the minimum costs points; wildly over wastes your time.
3. **In the oral, structure beats vocabulary.** An examiner would rather hear a clear *introduction → two arguments → conclusion* with simple words than brilliant vocabulary in chaos.
4. **Never leave a blank.** There is no negative marking.
5. **Learn the exam's magic phrases** — *"Je vais vous parler de…"*, *"Pour conclure…"* — they signal structure to the examiner instantly.
